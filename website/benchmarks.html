Benchmarks

Faster than HashLink's JIT

Across fib(40), Mandelbrot, n-body, inlined calls, method calls, and free-function calls, Ash's tiered JIT is measured against HashLink's released JIT, its in-development hl2-ir JIT, and its ahead-of-time HL/C output — same bytecode, every engine in a row timed on the same runner, every run checksum-verified. Numbers land fresh from CI with each push to main.

Every bar is the pure wall time of one run, verified against the benchmark's accepted checksums before it is published. Two kinds of row, marked AOT. The lanes tagged AOT — Ash AOT and HashLink/C — were compiled to a native binary before timing began, so their bars are this bytecode running as ordinary compiled code. The rest are still compiling inside the window being measured, which is the honest reason to read the two groups apart rather than as one ranking. haxe -hl main.c emits C for the system compiler to build; ash emits the object itself, with no C in the path. Both are built through LLVM — the HL/C lane is compiled with clang rather than the default cc, because ash's back end is LLVM by construction and leaving that unmatched would measure two compilers as much as two designs. Both builds take one to six seconds and are excluded from their bars, as is the Haxe JVM's; the VMs pay their compilation inside theirs. Each benchmark is measured in its own CI job, so the engines within a row share a machine but different rows may not — compare engines within a row, not milliseconds across sweeps.
